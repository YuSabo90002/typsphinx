<objective>
Establish, by measurement, why three ordinary kanji extract as NUL bytes from the Read the Docs-served
Japanese PDF — and put an evidenced set of fix options, with their scope consequences, in front of the
owner.

Purpose: the defect is real and specific — 10 literal NUL bytes replacing 発, 単 and 釈, on a PDF that
built green and downloaded cleanly — and its cause is genuinely unknown. Two explanations fit the
extraction evidence equally well and point at opposite fixes: a glyph that is absent from whatever font
Typst selected, or a glyph that paints correctly over a broken `ToUnicode` CMap. Plan 07 collected the
human observation that discriminates them; this plan adds the machine forensics that name the specific
font and the container measurement that says whether the glyph was ever available. The 95-versus-94 page
count is treated as part of the same question, because substituted font metrics reflowing a document by
one page is a plausible single mechanism for both symptoms and is testable.

The fix is deliberately NOT chosen here. Every candidate lever trades differently against milestone
invariant #3 and against Phase 29's already-verified English artifact, and that trade belongs to the
owner.

Output: `30.1-EVIDENCE.md` sections carrying the re-measured NUL map, per-occurrence font attribution,
the container's own font inventory, the page-count reconciliation, the English-parent measurement, a
stated root cause, and a decision block enumerating the options.

**Autonomy note:** `autonomous: false` because Task 3 ends in a blocking decision. Tasks 1 and 2 are fully
machine-executable and run first; the owner is asked to decide only once there is something measured to
decide about.
</objective>

- **Noun that is now primary:** *the CJK font used by the Japanese PDF*. Until now this phase treated the
  font as an outcome — whatever Typst's fallback happened to select from whatever the build container
