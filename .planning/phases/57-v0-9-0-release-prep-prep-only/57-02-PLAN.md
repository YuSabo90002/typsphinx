## Why this plan has `depends_on: []` — a refuted plan-checker BLOCKER

The plan-checker raised a BLOCKER asking that `57-01`, `57-03` and `57-04` be given
`depends_on: ["57-02"]`, on the reasoning that a plan-number-ordered sequential run would execute
`57-01`'s version bump before this plan's dispatch and trip its Step 1 guard. **Refuted by
measurement at plan time — do not "fix" it:**

- `workflow.use_worktrees` is `true`, `worktree.baseRef` is `"head"`, and `worktree base-check`
  returns `{"shouldDegrade": false, "reason": "baseref-head"}`. Worktree isolation is active and does
  not degrade on this branch. `CLAUDE.md` § "Worktree-isolated execution" records it as the project
  owner's **standing** execution mode.
- Under it every Wave-1 plan gets its own worktree branched at the phase head, and each worktree's
  commits merge back at wave cleanup — **after** the wave. `57-01`'s bump therefore cannot be present
  in this worktree's HEAD, which is exactly what the precondition asserts.
- `files_modified` do not overlap: `57-01` touches `pyproject.toml`, `README.md`, `uv.lock` and three
  planning artifacts; this plan touches only `57-CI-EVIDENCE.md`.
- The suggested dependency edges would **serialize Wave 1 for no gain** — worktrees branch from the
  phase head regardless of intra-wave order, so the edges change scheduling and nothing else.

**What actually protects run 1 is this plan's own Step 1 guard**, and it is deliberately more general
than the proposed dependency edges: it refuses to dispatch whenever the tree already carries this
phase's edits, for *any* cause — a degraded non-worktree runtime, a manual re-run, or a resumed
phase — not merely for the one ordering the checker imagined. Keep the guard; keep `depends_on: []`.
